# Semantic space addressing

A model does not encounter a linguistic expression as an empty identifier. Its training gives it associations, distinctions and patterns of use. MeSH’lAng treats those learned priors as something a deliberately defined language can address.

An expression can activate a semantic direction, bind it to another direction and place the resulting movement into a context. The intended result is a functional orientation that an agent can interpret and act upon.

## Composable semantic spectra

MeSH’lAng approaches semantic vector spaces through different **spectra**: selected ranges of associations, process relationships and operations that a language form makes addressable. The emphasis can be mathematical construction, process orientation, explicit workflow or the interpretive frame selected through a writing system.

crY, dRy, moQ’eX and the Bry exploration address different aspects of this design space. The aim is to compose such addresses within a shared task: a workflow expression can organize the work, a process expression can orient an agent's movement, and a mathematical expression can specify a construction. The language/profile and the common referents bind each part to its intended role.

This is an architectural composition principle. Each included exercise states which language forms it actually uses; the family description does not supply a universal parser or automatic cross-language operator mapping. See [the naming lineage](names.md#language-names-and-capitalization) and [the context contract](process-and-context-contract.md).

## The address and its interpretation

The approach distinguishes:

1. **The expression:** the symbol sequence and its language-specific syntax.
2. **The prior:** the associations available to the interpreting model.
3. **The binding:** the defined relationship between operators and directional elements.
4. **The context:** the task and scope in which the expression is applied.
5. **The unfolding:** the explicit interpretation and resulting action or proposal.

An invariant is the functional relationship intended to survive this unfolding. Agents can use different words while preserving the same relationship. Conversely, identical wording alone does not establish that their subsequent actions preserve it.

## Why a shared writing system matters

Devanagari is a writing system used for Sanskrit and modern languages including Hindi, Marathi and Nepali. It is a script, rather than the name of a single language. See the [Unicode Standard's Devanagari description](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-12/).

The choice of Devanagari is **aesthetic and pragmatic**:

- **Aesthetics:** a visual form the developer wanted for the notation.
- **Pragmatism:** a distinct interpretive frame whose operative meanings are explicitly bound by the primer. The aim is to reduce competing readings from everyday language and computing terminology, informed by the behavior of the examined models.

The choice has no religious purpose in this work. Meanings follow the selected language profile; the notation is not presented as a Sanskrit translation. Token load was considered as a practical compromise and is documented separately in the [token profile](../research/token-load-20261101.md).

Defining a language makes the selected reading and composition explicit. This implementation is one possible construction of semantic addressing.

## Shared attention and inference patterns

The architectural aim is to give agents a defined process-reading frame for the objects they jointly examine. A primer selects operative readings from learned priors; a chain directs attention within the relevant task and scope. The developer describes the resulting recurring interpretations and process choices as an inference pattern. The proposed advantage is more stable correlation and more explicit analysis of differences than ordinary prose provides. This is the architecture thesis, distinct from the narrower measured outcomes.

A gloss makes contextual unfolding visible at the operator surface. The intended agent-mesh exchange omits a repeated gloss when the necessary context is already shared. The [shared-context account](shared-context-communication.md) describes both modes, the interpretive-landscape rationale and conditional primer reuse.

## What “latent” means here

Latent space addressing describes the proposed interpretive mechanism: a symbolic expression recruits learned semantic structure. This publication does not expose model weights or identify a shared numeric coordinate system between providers. Its experiments examine observable interpretations, contextual shifts and functional preservation.

## Across agents and sessions

For another agent to resume an expression, it needs the expression and the relevant language and context bindings. Persisting those items allows the State and Trajectory to be revisited, compared and correlated. Geotopie and the Webstuhl belong to the surrounding architecture for storing, relating and examining such traces.

The expression, its contextual interpretation and evidence of its actual use are separate records. A persistent trace enables comparison; agreement is evaluated against the intended relationships.

The [process and context contract](process-and-context-contract.md) states what a handoff must bind. The [language/memory/runtime map](language-memory-runtime.md) separates the language from its surrounding implementation. The [research overview](../research/overview.md) and [semantic reproducibility protocol](../research/semantic-reproducibility.md) introduce the evidence and evaluation questions.

## Naming selects a semantic space

The deliberately retained German name Webstuhl is an example of this design choice. English weaving terms such as thread already have established computing meanings. Choosing a coherent German naming family helps select the intended architectural analogy and avoid competing IT associations. See the [Webstuhl case study](../case-studies/webstuhl.md).
